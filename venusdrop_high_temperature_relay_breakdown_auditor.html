Solid-State Breaker Physics in Supercritical Venusian Atmospheres

Detailed electro-thermal and quantum carrier generation models governing 4H-SiC junction field effect transistors at $465^\circ\text{C}$ (738 K) under 90 atm dense $CO_2$.

1. Intrinsic Carrier Explosion & Bandgap Shrinkage

While Silicon (Si) fails completely beyond $175^\circ\text{C}$ due to intrinsic carrier runaway ($n_i \approx N_D$), 4H-Silicon Carbide maintains wide-bandgap properties ($E_g \approx 3.26\text{ eV}$ at 300 K). However, at Venus surface conditions ($738\text{ K}$), the bandgap shrinks via the Varshni empirical relation, triggering exponential thermal generation across the depletion barrier:

$$E_g(T) = E_g(0) - \frac{\alpha T^2}{T + \beta}, \quad n_i(T) = \sqrt{N_c N_v} \exp\left(-\frac{E_g(T)}{2 k_B T}\right)$$

Where $\alpha = 3.3 \times 10^{-4}\text{ eV/K}$, $\beta = 0\text{ K}$, and $N_c, N_v$ scale as $T^{3/2}$. At $465^\circ\text{C}$, $n_i$ rises by over 11 orders of magnitude relative to room temperature, dramatically elevating thermal generation-recombination currents in reverse-biased p-n junction gates.

2. High-Temperature JFET Pinch-Off Mechanics

A normally-on SiC-JFET conducts through an n-type channel of metallurgical thickness $2a$ and donor concentration $N_D$. To interrupt fault currents or isolate buses, gate drivers apply a reverse bias $V_{GS} < 0$. The depletion width $W_d$ must bridge the metallurgical channel to establish isolation:

$$W_d(T) = \sqrt{\frac{2 \varepsilon_s (V_{bi}(T) - V_{GS})}{q N_D}}, \quad V_p(T) = \frac{q N_D a^2}{2 \varepsilon_s} - V_{bi}(T)$$

As built-in potential $V_{bi}(T) = \frac{k_B T}{q} \ln\left(\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i(T)^2}\right)$ drops steeply with increasing temperature, the effective pinch-off threshold shifts toward more negative potentials, demanding increased gate holding drive.

3. Closed-Loop Gate Driver Duty Cycle Inversion

To prevent continuous gate oxide stress (as seen in MOSFET failures), normally-on JFET drivers use a high-frequency switching inductor or pulsed charge pump to sustain negative bias. Reverse leakage $I_{\text{leak}}$ rapidly discharges the gate capacitance $C_{iss}$, mandating a closed-loop dynamic duty cycle $D(T)$:

$$I_{\text{leak}}(T) = q A \frac{W_d(T) n_i(T)}{2 \tau_0} + q A \frac{D_p n_i(T)^2}{L_p N_D}, \quad D_{\text{opt}}(T) = \frac{I_{\text{leak}}(T) \cdot \Delta t}{C_{iss} \Delta V_{GS}} + D_0$$

If $D(T)$ exceeds the safe thermal pinch threshold $D_{\text{crit}}(T)$, the driver circuits dissipate excess localized power ($P_{\text{gate}} = D \cdot V_{GS} \cdot I_{\text{leak}}$), initiating catastrophic latch-up runaway across the breaker module.

4. Venus Supercritical Fluid Boundary Cooling

At $90\text{ atm}$ and $465^\circ\text{C}$, the Venusian atmosphere acts as a supercritical fluid with density $\rho \approx 65\text{ kg/m}^3$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu \approx 3.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$. Heat transfer away from the ceramic circuit package is governed by high-Rayleigh natural convection:

$$\text{Ra}_L = \frac{g \beta_T (T_s - T_\infty) L^3}{\nu \alpha}, \quad \text{Nu}_L = 0.59 (\text{Ra}_L)^{1/4}, \quad h = \frac{\text{Nu}_L k_{CO2}}{L}$$

Dense $CO_2$ exhibits superior heat transfer ($h \approx 180\text{ W/m}^2\text{K}$) compared to Earth ambient air, but internal sealed electronics bays experience severe thermal soaking with zero internal convection unless actively ventilated.
